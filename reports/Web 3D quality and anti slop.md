# Bake the quality in before pixelating

Sniffer can match or beat itshoss.fun with free tools only. Treat HOSS's PS2-style filter as the last stage of a much better pipeline, not as the look itself. The plan has six parts. Build the mascot procedurally in headless Blender (bpy 5.3) rather than through free image-to-3D. Shade it as a clearcoat vinyl toy with baked AO and a ramp-plus-rim model. Reuse HOSS's CC-BY police cruiser and urban car pack, plus one CC0 city kit, all re-materialed to one palette. Replace the flat green circle sprites with toon-shaded, noise-displaced instanced "puff-balls" plus normal-mapped, alpha-eroded billboards. Then reimplement the retro chain (about 520-line internal resolution, nearest upscale, DoF gather, quarter-res bloom, 55/45 luminance-preserving/ACES grade, warm streaks, CRT glitch, CSS grain) as custom effects in **pmndrs/postprocessing on WebGLRenderer**. Finally, check every iteration with a separate evaluator agent that screenshots the page through Playwright under SwiftShader and scores it against a 3D-specific rubric. The research supports this split. The tools that raise rendered quality are the three.js graphics skills (scottstts, CloudAI-X), the Blender skill packs and the asset pipeline. The "anti-slop" tools (Impeccable, Hallmark, Taste Skill, ECC's design-system) only police 2D page chrome, and none of them detects 3D slop. The biggest risk is not tooling but evaluation. Without a skeptical, reference-anchored judge, the agent will praise its own primitive-built output.

## The 520-line filter is a pixel budget, not a quality ceiling

HOSS's filter discards most of the screen's pixels. Rendering at roughly 924×520 and upscaling with nearest filtering is about **4.3× fewer pixels than 1080p** (a calculation, not a measurement). That changes which effects are affordable on phones and on a CPU-only SwiftShader capture loop. Effects the notes rate as mobile-expensive become cheap at internal resolution. **N8AO** publishes about 0.70 ms at 1080p for its Medium preset on an Apple M5 Max and "generally 2x to 4x speedup" in halfRes mode. It must run through `N8AOPostPass` with pmndrs/postprocessing, and it requires WebGL2 ([N8python/n8ao](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao)). At 520 lines it should be cheap on phones, though nobody has published mobile timings. What the filter cannot hide is bad source material. Silhouettes, banded lighting, grounded contact shadows and controlled color all survive pixelation. Muddy tone, grey default materials and flat sprites survive it too.

The renderer should stay on **WebGLRenderer, three r187, built with Vite**. three's own manual still calls WebGPURenderer experimental and recommends WebGLRenderer for pure WebGL2 apps ([three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer)). Firefox for Android still has no WebGPU ([web-features explorer](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/webgpu/)). Android reports show WebGPU running at roughly half WebGL's frame rate in many-draw scenes, although those reports are against older revisions ([#29580](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/29580), [#30560](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/30560)). This decision also settles the post stack. **pmndrs/postprocessing** (3,000★, pushed 2026-11-22) "automatically organizes and merges any combination of effects" into one fullscreen pass ([pmndrs/postprocessing](https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing)), and three's EffectComposer cannot do that. pmndrs has no WebGPU support and N8AO doesn't support WebGPU yet, so committing to WebGL keeps both available. three's TSL display folder now ships `RetroPassNode`, `PixelationPassNode`, `CRT`, `DepthOfFieldNode` and `BloomNode` ([three.js tsl/display](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/dev/examples/jsm/tsl/display)). These are useful as readable references for a clean-room reimplementation of the HOSS chain, but they are WebGPU-path code, so the shipped versions should be custom GLSL `Effect` subclasses.

The retro chain maps onto this stack as follows:

1. Render the scene into a render target about 520 lines tall, with a depth texture.
2. Run N8AO at that resolution.
3. Run a DoF gather pass that reads that depth.
4. Downsample to quarter-res for bloom. Make neon, headlights, police bars and the gas's toxic band emissive above 1.0 so bloom stays selective.
5. Apply the 55/45 blend of luminance-preserving grade and ACES, then the warm streaks and the CRT glitch.
6. Upscale with nearest filtering to the canvas, and leave the grain to a CSS overlay.

The research notes push back on one part of HOSS's recipe. They argue that ACES "over-saturates neon" and that low-res bloom without a mip chain gives "blocky halos", and they would swap in AgX plus mipmap bloom. The user's decision to keep HOSS's grade stands. The 55% luminance-preserving branch is exactly the mitigation for ACES hue shift. The blockiness of quarter-res bloom is on-style once the whole frame is pixelated anyway. The practical takeaway is to keep emissive hues controlled, with lime gas pushed toward yellow-green, and to add a LUT after the grade if the dusk needs tuning. Tone mapping must happen inside the post chain, because `renderer.toneMapping` is ignored when rendering to a render target ([pmndrs ToneMappingMode docs](https://pmndrs.github.io/postprocessing/public/docs/file/src/enums/ToneMappingMode.js.html)). Leave SMAA off by default, since PS2-style stair-stepping is part of the look, and test it at internal resolution as an option.

Under the filter, the lighting recipe is bake-first:

- Bake AO and lightmaps for buildings and streets in Blender.
- Use a palette texture shared across the city, the approach of Bruno Simon's folio-2025 ([brunosimon/folio-2025](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2025)).
- Run one dusk HDRI through PMREM for car and board reflections.
- Use one directional shadow fitted tightly to the player, plus blob or contact shadows. Built-in `examples/jsm/csm` covers the desktop tier and replaces the stale three-csm ([three.js csm](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/dev/examples/jsm/csm)).
- Add depth fog toward the dusk sky using three's `Sky.js` ([three.js objects](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/dev/examples/jsm/objects)).

Shading should be a 3–4 band ramp with a sky-tinted Fresnel rim, not the stock toon material. Codrops' Ronin161 team found three's toon example "wasn't quite what we were looking for" and wrote their own ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2024/02/20/case-study-ronin161s-portfolio-2024/)). **THREE-CustomShaderMaterial** lets you inject that shading into built-in materials while keeping three's lights and shadows ([CustomShaderMaterial](https://github.com/FarazzShaikh/THREE-CustomShaderMaterial)). Its last push was 2025-10-12, so pin the version. Stay vanilla rather than R3F: R3F v10 is still alpha ([newreleases](https://newreleases.io/project/npm/@react-three/fiber/release/10.0.0-alpha.6)), and every technique here exists in vanilla three or drei-vanilla.

## A scripted Blender Sniffer beats free image-to-3D

With Meshy ruled out, the free image-to-3D route runs through Hugging Face ZeroGPU Spaces:

- **TRELLIS.2** (MIT, PBR GLB) at `microsoft/TRELLIS.2`.
- **Hunyuan3D-2.1** at `tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1`, which has a built-in 10k-face simplify and front/back/left/right input.
- **trellis-community/TRELLIS** for multi-image input.
- **SkinTokens** (MIT, UniRig's successor) for auto-rigging.

All four were running as of 2026-11-26 ([HF Spaces API](https://huggingface.co/api/spaces/microsoft/TRELLIS.2), [TRELLIS.2 app.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/microsoft/TRELLIS.2/blob/main/app.py), [Hunyuan3D-2.1 gradio_app.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1/blob/main/gradio_app.py), [SkinTokens model card](https://huggingface.co/VAST-AI/SkinTokens/blob/main/README.md)). The constraints are severe, though. Free accounts get **5 minutes of GPU per day**, and anonymous users get 2 ([HF ZeroGPU docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu.md)). TRELLIS.2 reserves 120 s for generation plus 120 s for GLB extraction, which works out to about **one textured generation per account per day**. The container's egress proxy also refused `tencent-hunyuan3d-2-1.hf.space` with HTTP 403. Every `*.hf.space` host has to be allowlisted by exact name, which needs the user to approve an environment change. The Hunyuan3D 2.x licence **excludes the EU, UK and South Korea** ([Hunyuan3D-2 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE)). The free Tripo tier bans commercial use ([Tripo commercial-use](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/commercial-use-ai-3d-models)).

The stronger position is that Sniffer doesn't need AI geometry at all. His design is a set of primitives: a teardrop hood with a bent tip, sphere goggles with torus rims, a cylinder snout with a grille, a torus belt with a box buckle, a flared robe shell and capsule feet. Free image-to-3D output arrives as 100k–500k triangles of triangle soup, with lighting baked into the textures. It then needs retopology, UVs and rebaking in Blender anyway. A bpy script gives a model that is natively 10–20k triangles, parametric for proportion tweaks, split into parts for per-part materials, and rig-friendly. The main weakness is organic drape on the hood and robe flare. To cover it, generate one TRELLIS.2 mesh from the front image, load it into the Blender scene as a silhouette reference, fit the procedural parts to it, and compare turntables. No published study compares procedural and AI mascots, so this verdict is engineering reasoning rather than a benchmark.

For rigging, skip Mixamo. It needs a humanoid "with distinguishable head, body, arm, and leg areas" and fails on large clothing ([Adobe Mixamo FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html)), and Sniffer's robe hides his legs. Script a small armature instead:

- Bones: root, body, head, a 3-bone hood-tip chain, arm_L/R and foot_L/R.
- Weights: automatic.
- Keyed clips: idle bob, lean left/right, ollie crouch-jump, wave and hood flop.
- Export: GLB with animations.

Drive the hood tip at runtime with a spring chain. SkinTokens is worth one free attempt as a comparison ([SkinTokens](https://huggingface.co/VAST-AI/SkinTokens/blob/main/README.md)). The materials do most of the "premium toy" work. Use a Principled clearcoat for vinyl, flat per-part colors and a baked AO lightmap. In three, that becomes MeshPhysicalMaterial clearcoat on the hero only, plus a sheen option for the robe.

Use plain bpy scripts rather than an MCP for the Blender work. **ahujasid/mcp-for-blender** (35,877★) needs a running Blender instance ([mcp-for-blender](https://github.com/ahujasid/mcp-for-blender)), and plain bpy scripts are reproducible in a GPU-less container. The skill pack to install is **EL4CTEO/blender-skills** (2,941★, pushed 2026-11-21). It has 28 skills covering modeling, UVs, PBR, baking, rigging, stylized looks, export and QA. It requires Blender 5.2 LTS or later and recommends **5.3, which "adds KTX2 web export"** ([EL4CTEO/blender-skills](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/blender-skills)). That matches the working bpy 5.3. **majidmanzarpour/blender-game-skills** contributes `blender-image-to-3d`, which builds rigged assets from model sheets "measured against your references" ([blender-game-skills](https://github.com/majidmanzarpour/blender-game-skills)). Use it for the reference-fit workflow. **arjun988/blender-skills** routes through Blender MCP and is not yet in the marketplace, so skip it. **instruktlabs/kiln** (local, keyless, returns rendered views plus structural checks and exports GLB) is a reasonable fallback for props if bpy work stalls ([kiln](https://github.com/instruktlabs/kiln)).

For cars and the city, the decisions are already made. Reuse HOSS's murphy.glb police cruiser (Daniel Zhabotinsky) and urban.glb "20 urban cars" (zairiq-123) under CC-BY. Sketchfab's rule is that attribution must travel with the asset, as the creator username plus the model link ([Sketchfab help](https://help.sketchfab.com/en/articles/16152215-crediting-users-for-3d-model-downloads)). That means a CREDITS file and an on-page credit line. Fill the city with one CC0 family: **Kenney** City Kit or Car Kit (CC0, glTF) ([Kenney Car Kit](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/car-kit), [City Kit](https://opengameart.org/content/city-kit-commercial)) or Quaternius (CC0) ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/lowpoly-cars)). Then re-material everything, the CC-BY cars included, onto one palette texture and one shading model. Mixing kit styles is the clearest way to look assembled rather than art-directed. Give police light bars emissive values above 1 so they feed bloom. Use clearcoat only on the hero cruiser.

Compress everything at build time with `gltf-transform optimize --compress meshopt --texture-compress ktx2`. Use ETC1S for albedo and UASTC for normals ([npm @gltf-transform/cli](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@gltf-transform/cli)). Verify the flags with `--help`. Load with `MeshoptDecoder` and `KTX2Loader`. Never mix gltfpack meshopt output with Draco. Draw city pieces as InstancedMesh or BatchedMesh with object pooling, targeting roughly 100–150 draw calls on phones. Nearest-filtered textures at 520 lines show little above 512–1024 px, so the texture budget can be small.

## The gas wins on shading, not particle count

The current gas is instanced camera-facing quads with one radial-gradient canvas texture, and that is the canonical slop baseline. A recent tutorial still recommends exactly that approach ([Shane Brumback](https://www.shanebrumback.com/articles/threejs-smoke-effect.html)). The fix doesn't need a new engine. A trail needs at most a few thousand big puffs, and on phones the limit is overdraw, not simulation. The best fit is a tiered hybrid that shares one green toon ramp across three techniques.

For the **body** of trail clouds, the police-car envelope, the mega-fart burst and close-ups, use **toon puff-balls**. These are instanced icospheres at detail 2–3, displaced along the normal by time-animated 3D noise with a per-instance seed, so each reads as a lumpy cauliflower. Shade them with a 3-step green ramp (dark olive, toxic lime, pale yellow-green), a Fresnel rim and a slight emissive band. Dissolve them with a noise-thresholded `discard` and pop them in with an ease-out-back scale. Because they are alpha-tested opaque geometry, they need no sorting, write depth, can take an inverted-hull outline, and hold up when they fill the camera. The technique comes from cartoon-explosion practice in Blender ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/cartoon-explosions-tutorial/1175640)). CustomShaderMaterial fits it well on WebGLRenderer.

For **wisps**, the trail tail and the manhole steam, upgrade the billboards along the lines of McGuire and Fein's real-time cartoon smoke. Their puff textures carry normal maps that drive two-tone cel shading, and the outlines come from depth differences ([McGuire & Fein 2006](https://faculty.digipen.edu/~gherron/references/References/NPR/mf-2006-toonsmoke.pdf)). Add BotW-style alpha erosion: a hard `smoothstep` edge against `alpha × noise`, so puffs eat away crisply instead of fading to mush ([80.lv Zelda VFX](https://80.lv/articles/creating-stylized-combat-vfx-in-the-zelda-style/)). Add depth-fade soft particles and premultiplied normal blending. Free CC0 puff textures exist: Kenney "Smoke Particles" (70 files) ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/smoke-particles)), JangaFX VDB sims to render into flipbooks ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2022/04/download-15-free-smoke-fire-and-dust-sims-in-vdb-format)), and Unity Labs' CC0 flipbooks ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/free-vfx-image-sequences-flipbooks)). Blender can also bake displaced-sphere puffs with a sphere-normal RGB channel directly.

For the **full-screen transition**, use a domain-warped FBM threshold expanding from Sniffer's screen position, with stepped bands and an `fwidth` outline. It is practically free. As a richer option, port PavelDoGreat's 2D stable-fluids sim at 128–256 px and posterize the dye ([WebGL-Fluid-Simulation](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)). That repo is stale since 2024-11 and its licence was not verified.

The retro filter rewards this design. Two or three hard toon bands survive a 520-line nearest downsample. A soft radial gradient turns into a blotch. The DoF gather and soft particles can share the same internal-resolution depth texture. The toxic emissive band gives the quarter-res bloom something to catch. If the team wants an emitter library instead of a hand-rolled CPU sim, **three.quarks** (1,311★, pushed 2026-11-18) is the mature choice. It has soft particles, frame-blended flipbooks, bursts and trails, and v0.18 added a WebGPU/TSL batch renderer alongside the classic WebGL one ([three.quarks CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Alchemist0823/three.quarks/master/CHANGELOG.md)). Its editor is stale (2023), and you still write the custom material either way. Skip raymarched volumes (takram three-clouds is GLSL-only, tied to the pmndrs composer, and sky-scale ([README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@takram/three-clouds@0.7.4/README.md))) and Gaussian splats. They are expensive and off-style.

## Anti-slop tools police the page chrome; 3D needs a custom judge

Every visual anti-slop tool surveyed targets 2D web UI tells: purple gradients, Inter, cards inside cards, emoji labels. None of them flags default grey MeshStandardMaterial, missing tone mapping, sprite smoke or a capsule-and-sphere character. That 3D-specific rubric has to be written in-house. Within the 2D layer, the deterministic tools are the ones worth having in an agent loop, because their verdicts don't negotiate. **pbakaus/impeccable** has a detector with "62 deterministic rules… No LLM, no API key" that scans a rendered page, plus `critique`, `audit` and `polish` commands. Its README notes it is "strongest on interfaces that already exist" ([impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)), which suits a rebuild. **Anthropic's `frontend-design`** skill is the baseline taste guidance. It now names the current AI clusters to avoid, including near-black with an acid-green accent ([anthropics/skills frontend-design](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/tree/main/skills/frontend-design)). That is a live hazard for a green-fart brand, and the UI palette should come from Sniffer's art rather than defaulting there. For page-level art direction, pick either Taste Skill (scripted dealer plus a headless-Chrome checker at 5 widths ([taste-skill](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill))) or Hallmark ([hallmark](https://github.com/Nutlope/hallmark)), not both. **codeswithroh/tastemaker** extracts the palette from reference pixels and persists a style lock ([tastemaker](https://github.com/codeswithroh/tastemaker)), which helps anchor the UI to the mascot's colors. Skip ui-ux-pro-max (catalog-default output despite 154k stars), merged "design-taste" bundles, mirror registries, and the prose and code de-slop tools.

For rendered quality, install two three.js skill packs:

- **scottstts/Threejs-Awesome-Graphics-Agent-Skills** (1,030★). It "is NOT a three.js API cheat sheet". It includes `threejs-procedural-vfx`, `threejs-procedural-materials`, mesh-defect audits, and an operating model that demands "diagnostic outputs" and "a no-post baseline that still reads" ([scottstts](https://github.com/scottstts/Threejs-Awesome-Graphics-Agent-Skills)). That last rule is exactly the discipline the retro filter needs.
- **CloudAI-X/threejs-skills** (4,446★). It pins r186 APIs because models mix up renamed APIs ("RGBELoader became HDRLoader, Clock gave way to Timer") ([CloudAI-X](https://github.com/CloudAI-X/threejs-skills)).

Both packs lean toward WebGPU/TSL in places. Steer them to WebGL, and check which backend actually ran in headless Chromium. From the ECC marketplace the user added, enable only these: `gan-style-harness` with its `gan-planner`, `gan-generator` and `gan-evaluator` agents, `browser-qa`, `design-system` (with `slop-check`), `frontend-design-direction` and `loop-design-check`. ECC is 328 skills and has no three.js skill. Its `taste*` skills are about music-video color grading, not web design ([affaan-m/ECC](https://github.com/affaan-m/ECC)). Installing all of it degrades skill routing.

The critique loop should copy Anthropic's harness design. In that design a separate, skeptical evaluator drives the live page through Playwright MCP, scores weighted criteria calibrated with few-shot examples, and feeds 5–15 generator iterations. Scores plateau, and sometimes a middle iteration is best. Self-graders were found "confidently praising the work" when it was mediocre ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). ECC's gan-style-harness already implements this, with weights of Design Quality 0.3, Originality 0.2, Craft 0.3 and Functionality 0.2 and a 7.0 pass threshold. Rewrite the rubric for 3D:

- **Criteria:** silhouette readability, material and lighting, color and grade, gas VFX, composition, and fidelity to HOSS's filter.
- **Anchors:** the current site's frames as the "slop = 3/10" example and itshoss.fun captures as the bar.
- **Rule:** the evaluator lists the three worst visual defects before it gives any score.

The capture side works without a GPU. Use Playwright MCP (`--caps vision` for canvas) or a Playwright script, with Chromium's explicit SwiftShader flags `--use-gl=angle --use-angle=swiftshader-webgl --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`. Wait for an app-set `window.__frameReady` flag and check that the canvas is not blank ([microlink](https://microlink.io/blog/webgl-without-a-gpu), [playwright-mcp](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp)). Add **pixelmatch** diffs against the last accepted frame ([pixelmatch](https://github.com/mapbox/pixelmatch)). One addition specific to this project is to capture both the raw pre-filter buffer and the final filtered frame. A pixelating, grainy filter flatters everything, and judging only the filtered output lets weak models and lighting pass. The 520-line internal resolution also keeps SwiftShader capture fast enough for 5–10 rounds.

## Conclusion

The reference site's strength is not exotic rendering. It is a strong filter over decent assets. That means Sniffer wins by making the pre-filter frame good enough to pass the "no-post baseline that still reads" test, then letting the same filter style it. The free-only constraint costs little here. The step that free tools handle worst, AI character generation on a 5-minute daily quota, is also the one this particular mascot doesn't need. A parametric, part-separated bpy model ends up a better web asset than anything Meshy would have returned.

The real gap the research exposed is evaluation. The tools that ship deterministic anti-slop rules are all 2D, and LLM judges are known to be generous. A 3D-specific rubric, anchored to captured reference frames and applied to both the raw and filtered buffers, is the one piece the team has to author itself. It is also the piece most likely to decide whether the rebuild actually beats itshoss.fun rather than merely differing from it.
